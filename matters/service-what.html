What to track beyond clicks: calls, forms, and booked jobs

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 3, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

A click only tells you someone looked. Calls, form submissions, and booked jobs tell you someone wanted to hire you, and those are the numbers worth reporting on every week.

Start with phone calls

For most service businesses, calls are the main conversion. Google Ads can measure calls in several ways: calls made from your ads, calls made after someone visits your website, and calls to a number shown on a call asset. Calls from ads are counted in your Conversions column once call reporting is turned on.

Google also lets you set a minimum call length, so a five second hang up is not counted as a lead. When call recording is enabled, it can analyze recordings to judge lead quality. Recording is limited to calls where both parties are in the U.S. or Canada, so check the rules before you turn it on.

Count forms and booking clicks separately

Quote requests, contact forms, and online booking buttons each deserve their own conversion action. Keeping them separate shows which action your customers prefer and which campaigns produce them. A single blended "leads" number hides that.

Tag every lead with its source

A call is more useful when you know which ad, keyword, or listing produced it. Set up tracking so each call and form carries its source. Then you can compare cost per lead by campaign and move budget toward the searches that fill the schedule.

Go one step past the lead

Leads are not jobs. Keep a simple log of which leads became booked work and what each job was worth, even if it is a spreadsheet. Over a few months, it shows you the cost per booked job, which is the number that decides whether a campaign is worth continuing.

An illustrative example

An HVAC company sees a campaign report showing 400 clicks. That sounds healthy, but clicks include people who left immediately. When the company adds call tracking and a form conversion, the same month shows a smaller but real number of calls and requests, each tied to a keyword. Now the owner can see which searches brought real customers and which ones only brought traffic.

Common mistakes to avoid

Counting every click as a lead, counting very short calls as conversions, lumping calls and forms into one number, and never checking whether leads became jobs. Clean tracking is simple to set up and hard to fix later, so it pays to do it at the start.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, list every contact point and decide which ones count as a lead. Week two, set up call tracking and separate form conversions, with a minimum call length. Week three, test every path yourself: call the number from an ad, submit the form, and confirm each shows up in your reports. Week four, begin the job log, noting which leads booked and what the work was worth. By the end of the month you should be able to answer a simple question: what did we pay for each booked job, and from which source did it come?

Try this first

List every way a customer can contact you (phone, form, text, booking link). Check that each one is tracked as its own conversion, with the source attached.
