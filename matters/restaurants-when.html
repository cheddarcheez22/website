When to push: lunch, weekends, holidays, and new menus

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 16, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Promote the shifts that are slow, not the ones that are already full. With 60% of operators reporting softer traffic last year, filling the quiet hours is often worth more than adding to a busy Saturday.

Find your quiet hours

Google shows a popular times graph for many businesses, including how busy a location is through the day, typical visit length, and wait times. The data comes from aggregated and anonymized information from users who have opted in to location history, and it appears only when a business gets enough visits. Look at your own listing the way a guest sees it, then compare it with your point of sale data to see where you have room.

Schedule ads around real capacity

Google Ads lets you choose the days and hours your ads appear and adjust bids for specific times. Show ads for lunch when you want weekday lunch traffic, and for dinner when you want to add covers. Pause or reduce them at the hour you are already full.

Time your calendar moments

New menus, seasonal dishes, holidays, and local events give people a reason to act now. Plan them several weeks ahead so photos, listing updates, emails, and ads are ready together. A grand opening or launch night benefits from an even earlier start.

Match the message to the daypart

A weekday lunch message is about speed and value. A weekend dinner message is about occasion and groups. A late night message is about being open when others are not. Keep each one short and tied to one action, such as order, reserve, or get directions.

Review and repeat

After each push, compare the same weekday and daypart to the week before. Keep what filled seats, drop what did not.

An illustrative example

A cafe is packed from 8 to 10 a.m. but quiet between 1 and 4 p.m. Instead of promoting breakfast, the owner builds a small afternoon offer and schedules ads for those hours. After a few weeks, the afternoon is steadier, and the morning rush is unchanged. The owner did not need more demand overall, only better timing.

Common mistakes to avoid

Promoting your busiest shift, running ads when the kitchen cannot keep up, announcing a new menu the day it launches, and forgetting holiday hours. Plan the moment, announce it early, and make sure the operation can handle the response.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, review your sales by hour and weekday and mark the three weakest time blocks. Week two, design one offer or reason to visit for the weakest block and set ad schedules to match. Week three, launch it with a short email or text to your list and one post. Week four, compare that block with the same block the month before and decide whether to keep, change, or stop. Add a calendar of upcoming menu launches and holidays so each one has enough preparation time.

Try this first

Find your three slowest daypart and weekday combinations. Build your next campaign around the first one.
