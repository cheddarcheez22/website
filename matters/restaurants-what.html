What to track: calls, directions, reservation clicks, and online orders

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 11, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Before a guest walks in, they usually tap something: call, directions, menu, reserve, or order. Those taps are measurable, free to view, and the clearest early signal that your marketing is working.

Start with your Google Business Profile

Google's Business Profile performance report shows calls (taps on your call button), directions requests, website clicks, bookings when you use a booking provider, messages, and the search terms people used to find you. Menus and offers appear for businesses that have them. Check it monthly and write the numbers down so you can compare month to month.

Add ad level tracking

If you run Google Ads, local actions conversions record what people do on your location listing after interacting with an ad: directions, clicks to call, website visits, and, for businesses that offer them, orders and menu views. Google counts these within a 30 day window by default and includes them in your All conversions column. This shows which campaigns send people toward your door, not just to your website.

Track the actions that make money

Not every tap is equal. A reservation click or order click is closer to revenue than a menu view. Give the high value actions their own line in your report so a spike in menu views does not hide a drop in orders.

Build a weekly scorecard

Keep it to six lines: calls, directions, website clicks, reservation clicks, online orders, and new reviews. Review it every week, and note what you changed (a new menu, a promotion, an ad update) beside the number. After a couple of months, patterns appear.

An illustrative example

A restaurant changes its photos and menu on its Google profile, then watches the weekly scorecard. Directions requests rise the following week, but calls stay flat. The owner learns that the new photos help people find the place, while phone ordering depends on something else, such as a clearer call button or longer hours. Without the scorecard, both effects would be invisible.

Common mistakes to avoid

Checking numbers only when sales are down, treating menu views as sales, forgetting to record what changed each week, and ignoring tracking on third party ordering links. If a click leaves your site without a trace, you cannot learn from it.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, set up the weekly scorecard and record the last four weeks from your Google profile. Week two, make sure your reservation and ordering links are the ones you want to count and that your phone number and menu are current. Week three, if you run ads, check that local actions are being recorded and that you can separate them by campaign. Week four, review the scorecard and note one change to try next month. Keep the habit small enough that you actually do it every week. If you share the sheet with your team, they will start noticing the connection between what they do on shift and what shows up in the numbers.

Try this first

Open your Google Business Profile performance report and record this month's calls, directions, and website clicks. Then set up the same three numbers on a weekly scorecard.
