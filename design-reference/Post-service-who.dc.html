Who is actually searching: homeowners, landlords, and urgent buyers

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 9, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

The person searching for a plumber at 9 p.m. is not the same buyer as a landlord with ten units or a homeowner planning a remodel. Knowing which group you want changes your keywords, your ad copy, and your follow up.

Homeowners and renters are different markets

The Census Bureau reported a homeownership rate of 65.5% in the third quarter of 2026. That means roughly two in three households own their homes and about one in three rent. Owners make decisions about replacements, upgrades, and long term repairs. Renters usually contact a landlord or property manager first, which creates a second kind of customer.

Landlords and property managers buy differently

A property manager is rarely a one time buyer. They care about response time, clear invoicing, and reliability across many units. Messaging that speaks to repeat work, and a simple way to get on a vendor list, will reach them better than a promotion aimed at a single homeowner.

Urgent buyers decide fast

Someone with no hot water searches on a phone, calls the first credible result, and does not compare prices. Invoca found that 62% of consumers call before making a purchase. These buyers respond to fast answers, visible hours, and clear signs you will actually show up.

Match the message to the buyer

Separate your campaigns or ad groups by intent. Use emergency language and a call button for urgent searches. Use proof, financing options, and project examples for planned work. Use reliability and repeat service language for property managers. Then track each group's cost per lead separately.

An illustrative example

A landscaping company advertises one general "free quote" message to everyone. The calls it gets are a mix of one time cleanups and property managers asking about monthly service. After the company splits its ads into two groups, with one focused on single projects and one on ongoing maintenance for properties, it can see which group costs more to win and which one stays longer.

Common mistakes to avoid

Writing one message for every buyer, ignoring property managers because they seem harder to reach, and assuming that urgent buyers want a discount when they mostly want speed. Describe the buyer first, then write the ad.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, list your last twenty jobs and label each buyer as owner, landlord or property manager, or urgent caller. Week two, write one short message for each group and one clear action, such as call now, request a quote, or join our vendor list. Week three, split your campaigns or ad groups to match and track results for each. Week four, compare cost per lead and job value by group. Put more budget behind the group that gives you the best jobs, not simply the cheapest leads. Revisit the split each quarter, since the mix of buyers shifts with the season and with the neighborhoods you serve.

Try this first

Write down the three best jobs you booked last quarter. Note who called, why, and how they found you. That tells you which buyer to build around first.
