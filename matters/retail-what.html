What to track: store visits, calls, directions, and online orders

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 28, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

You do not need a complicated dashboard. Calls, directions, website clicks, and orders, reviewed weekly, show whether your marketing is moving people toward your store.

Use what Google already gives you

The Business Profile performance report shows calls, directions requests, website clicks, messages, and search terms. For stores with products or offers listed, those appear too. It is free, and it is the closest thing to a daily pulse for a local shop.

Add ad level counts

Google Ads local actions conversions count what people do on your location listing after an ad interaction: directions, clicks to call, website visits, and, for businesses that offer them, orders and menu views. The default window is 30 days, and the results appear in the All conversions column. Store visit conversions are a related but separate feature with its own eligibility requirements, so check the Google Ads Help before assuming they are available to you.

Count online orders separately

If you sell online, track orders on your website as their own conversion. Compare them to in store activity so you can see whether an ad drives people to buy online, visit the shop, or both.

Build a four line scorecard

Calls, directions, website clicks, and orders. Keep it weekly. Add a note for anything you changed: a window display, a promotion, a new product, an ad edit. After two months, the notes explain the numbers.

Tie it to sales

Even a rough link between those counts and weekly revenue is useful. If directions rise before a good sales week more than once, you have found a leading indicator.

An illustrative example

An optical store keeps a short sheet taped beside the register. Each Monday, the owner copies four numbers from Google: calls, directions, website clicks, and appointments booked online. A short note records what changed that week, such as a window sign or a new frame line. After two months, the owner can see that appointment bookings jump after email campaigns, and calls rise after new photos.

Common mistakes to avoid

Tracking too many numbers, forgetting to note changes, comparing weeks with different holidays, and ignoring online orders or appointment links. Four consistent numbers beat twenty that nobody reads.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, create the four line scorecard and fill in the last month. Week two, confirm your website orders and appointment links are tracked as their own conversions. Week three, add a note column for changes and enter every update you make. Week four, review the month and choose one number to improve next month. Keep the sheet in a place where whoever opens the shop each Monday can reach it in a minute. The habit matters more than the tool, and a paper sheet works as well as a spreadsheet if it gets filled in every week. Revisit it each quarter and retire any number that has stopped teaching you something.

Try this first

Create a one page weekly sheet with calls, directions, website clicks, and online orders. Fill in the last four weeks from your Google reports.
