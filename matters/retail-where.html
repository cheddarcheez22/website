Where shoppers look: local search, maps, and social

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 29, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Most shoppers start on Google, use Maps to find the way, and check social media for a feel for the shop. If your information is wrong in any of them, you can lose the visit.

Google leads local search

BrightLocal's 2025 survey of 1,000 US adults found that 45% default to Google for local searches (53% including Safari users), 15% default to Google Maps, and 14% default to social media as a group. Among Gen Z, Google is at 30%, Google Maps at 18%, and TikTok at 10%. Your shop needs to show up well in all three, with an emphasis on whichever your customers use most.

Hours and contact details decide visits

In the same survey, 85% of consumers said contact information and opening hours are important when choosing a local business, and 46% called them very important. For a store, that is a practical point: customers will not walk in if they are not sure you are open. Update holiday hours early and keep your phone number current.

Consistency builds trust

Another 56% often or always check that a business's information is accurate and consistent across sources. Compare your name, address, hours, and category on Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, and your own site. Fix differences.

Show the shop

Add recent photos of the storefront, the inside, and popular products to your profile. Shoppers who plan a route, as 49% do, want to recognize the place when they arrive.

Use social media for discovery

Short videos and photos of new arrivals, displays, and staff picks help people find you and decide to visit. Point each post to one action: visit, call, or order.

An illustrative example

A shopper in a new neighborhood searches for a card store on a phone, opens Maps, and sees three options. One lists hours that ended an hour ago. One has no photos. One has current hours, five clear pictures, and a cluster of recent reviews. The third gets the visit. Nothing about the product mattered yet. Accuracy and presentation made the choice.

Common mistakes to avoid

Letting holiday hours go stale, using a logo as the only photo, leaving questions unanswered, and neglecting directory sites that feed other apps. A quick monthly check usually catches most of these problems.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, audit your name, hours, phone, and category on every platform where you appear. Week two, replace weak photos with a set that shows the storefront, the inside, and a few products. Week three, add holiday hours and answer any questions customers have posted. Week four, post a short video or set of photos on social media that points to one action. Repeat the audit monthly. A shopper who finds correct information right away is far more likely to walk in. It is a small habit, and it keeps the basics right during the weeks when you are too busy to notice.

Try this first

Check your hours on Google, Apple Maps, and Yelp today. Add holiday hours for the coming season and upload five fresh photos.
