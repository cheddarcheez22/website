When to spend: holidays, openings, and local events

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 30, 2026

The winter holiday season is the biggest spending window of the year for most retailers, and openings and local events create smaller waves of their own. Spend more where demand already exists and prepare early.

The holiday season sets the pace

The National Retail Federation forecasts that 2026 holiday sales will grow 4.2% over 2025 to $1.013 trillion for November 1 through December 31, excluding automobile dealers, gasoline stations, and restaurants. Last year's holiday sales rose 3.1% to $972 billion. The NRF also describes consumers as prioritizing the season while staying mindful of their budgets, so clear value matters.

Prepare before the rush

Update your hours and photos, load your best products and offers into your listing, and have your emails and ads ready before the season begins. Check that your phone number and ordering links work. A small error during a peak week is expensive.

Use ad scheduling and budgets

Google Ads lets you choose the days and hours your ads run and adjust bids by time. During peak weeks, favor the hours when shoppers are most likely to visit or order, and keep enough daily budget to avoid running out early in the day.

Treat openings and events as campaigns

A grand opening, a new product launch, or a local event needs its own timeline: announce it, remind people, and follow up. Local community calendars and neighborhood events are good places to tie in.

Plan the quiet months too

Not every month is a peak. Use slower periods to collect emails, refresh your listing, and test new messages, so you go into the next busy season knowing what works.

An illustrative example

A local shop plans for the holidays in October. It updates photos, builds a short gift guide, sets early and late hours, and schedules email reminders for the two weeks before major shopping days. When the busy week arrives, there is little to fix. A competitor that waited until the first big weekend spends the key days correcting basic details.

Common mistakes to avoid

Starting preparation at the peak, cutting ad budgets in the middle of the season, forgetting inventory limits, and ignoring the quiet weeks that follow. Preparation costs little compared with a missed weekend.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, list your biggest sales weeks and the events around them. Week two, build a calendar for the next quarter that includes holidays, openings, and local events, with preparation dates two to three weeks earlier. Week three, update your listing, photos, and offers for the first date on the calendar. Week four, set ad schedules and budgets and check that nothing will run out early. After each event, record what worked so next year starts from a better plan. Share the calendar with your team so everyone knows which weeks are all hands and which are for catching up.

Try this first

Write down your three biggest sales weeks from last year. For each, list what you will have ready two weeks beforehand: hours, photos, offers, and ads.
