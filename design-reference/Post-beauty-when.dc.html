When to promote: seasonal treatments, new services, and slow weekdays

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 23, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Promotions work best when they match demand you can already see. Your Business Profile search terms, booking calendar, and ad reports show when clients look for each service, and when your chairs are empty.

Read what clients searched

Google's Business Profile performance report lists the search terms people used to find your business. The data updates at the start of each month. Review the same months over a few years and you will see which services rise and fall through the year, then plan promotions a few weeks before the rise.

Fill the slow days first

Look at your booking calendar for the emptiest weekdays and times. Build offers and reminders for those slots rather than adding demand to times that already fill. Google Ads lets you schedule ads by day and hour and adjust bids by time, which helps you send traffic toward the days you want to fill.

Launch new services with a runway

A new treatment or location needs lead time. Start updating your listing, website, and photos before launch. Collect interest in advance with a waiting list or an announcement email. Run a short campaign that points to one booking action.

Time your review requests

BrightLocal's 2025 survey found that healthcare and beauty clients generally expect a review request within three days to a week of their visit. Send it while the experience is fresh, and after any result is visible.

Time follow ups to the treatment

Many treatments are repeated on a schedule. Set a reminder for each service based on your providers' recommendations, so clients hear from you when it is useful to them.

An illustrative example

A salon notices that Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons are always open. It creates a small weekday offer for a popular service, advertises it only to people near the salon during those hours, and emails past clients a week ahead. The Tuesday and Wednesday calendar fills while weekends stay full at regular prices. The offer was timed to a gap, not spread across the week.

Common mistakes to avoid

Discounting at busy times, launching a service without preparing the listing, asking for reviews weeks after the visit, and ignoring last year's search terms. Your own calendar and search history are better guides than any generic seasonal chart.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, review your Business Profile search terms and your booking calendar by weekday. Week two, pick one slow window and one service that fits it, and build a small offer or reminder. Week three, run it for two weeks with ad schedules that favor your target days. Week four, measure bookings in the target window against the same window a month earlier. Keep a running calendar of promotions and results so next year you can start earlier and with more confidence. Share the calendar with your front desk so they can offer open times to the clients most likely to want them.

Try this first

Pull the last 12 months of bookings by weekday. Choose your slowest weekday and design one offer or reminder to fill it next month.
